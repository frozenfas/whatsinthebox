Getting What's in the Box working on your phone

Seven steps, about ten minutes. This covers photographing and finding ordinary things you pack, plus getting both our phones on the same list — nothing about coins here, that's a separate thing you can ignore entirely.

1

Open the app and install it

In Chrome, open this address:

frozenfas.github.io/whatsinthebox Open

Chrome will offer Add to Home screen — either as a banner at the bottom, or under the ⋮ menu in the top right. Tap it and confirm. This puts a proper icon on your home screen so it opens full-screen, like a normal app.

You only do this once.

2

Add the identification key

Open the app from your home screen, then go to the Setup tab. Under Identification, paste in the API key — I'll send this to you separately. Tap Save.

This is what lets the app recognise what's in each photo. Without it, photos just sit there unidentified.

3

Say who's entering things

Still on Setup, under About you, type your name and save. It gets stamped on locations you create, so we can tell who packed what later.

4

Connect the two phones

Still on Setup, under Sync between phones: enter the shared sync passphrase — same one on both phones, I'll send it to you separately, not here — and tap Save passphrase. It scrambles everything before it ever leaves your phone, so keep it somewhere you won't lose, like your password manager.

Then tap Connect OneDrive and sign in with the shared account I'll also send you separately — not your own personal Microsoft account. Both phones use that same sign-in, which is what lets them share one list.

After that, tap Sync now any time you want the two catalogues to catch up with each other — it also happens on its own whenever you open the app with a connection.

Sign in with the shared account, not your own — using your personal Microsoft account here would try to sync into the wrong place.
5

Set up a location

Go to the Locations tab. At the top, pick or create the house you're actually standing in — everything below only shows locations at that house, since you can't be packing at both at once.

Then give a new location a name (a box, a shelf, a cupboard — whatever it actually is) and a colour, and tap Create box. Print its label if you've got a printer handy, or just remember which one you're using for now.

6

Photograph things as you pack

Capture tab, big camera button in the middle. One object per photo. It'll save instantly and identify itself in the background — you don't need to wait, just keep photographing and packing.

If something's a coin or a book, a "+ Add another photo" button appears once it's identified — that's optional extra detail for those two categories only. Everything else just needs the one photo.
7

Find things later

Find tab, search by name, material, or any text printed on the object. This searches both houses at once, and tells you which one each result is in.

Everything you photograph stays on your phone, and syncs to the shared account once step 4 is done — never to your own personal OneDrive. Nothing about coins, Numista, or the scale card matters for normal use — skip all of it.
